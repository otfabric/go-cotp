# Limitations

Nothing in this library is certified, and no conformance test suite has been run. Requirement-by-requirement status against X.214, X.224, RFC 1006 and RFC 2126 is in [docs/COMPLIANCE.md](docs/COMPLIANCE.md); this page is the summary a user needs before relying on the library.

## Not implemented

- Transport classes other than 0 as a service. Classes 1 to 4 exist at most as wire structures: no state machine, flow control, multiplexing or class 4 checksum.
- TP2 and the rest of RFC 2126 beyond the Class 0 subset.
- An expedited data service. ED and EA are codec only, and the library's ED follows X.224, not the RFC 1006 form.
- Graceful release: a TP0 connection ends by closing the stream. A DR in the open state aborts it, with no DC.
- Extended formats. A DT whose LI is odd is the extended class 2 to 4 format (X.224 §13.7.4 NOTE: an even LI is the normal format, an odd LI the extended one); `Decode` and `DecodeDT` reject it with `ErrUnsupportedDTVariant`. ED, AK and EA are decoded in their normal format only: the codec has no check that tells their extended formats apart.
- Class and alternative-class negotiation, QoS, and the additional-options parameters.
- Native CONS and CLNS transports.

Reconnect, discovery, dialling, TLS configuration and application protocols are out of scope by design ([docs/ARCHITECTURE.md](docs/ARCHITECTURE.md)).

## Behaviour to know

- A peer SRC-REF of 0 is rejected. Whether this suits real PLCs has not been checked; [testdata/tp0/README.md](testdata/tp0/README.md) records that the installed S7 base often sends 0.
- Segmentation follows X.224 §13.7, not the 65524-octet figure in RFC 1006; the discrepancy is entry E-09 in [docs/COMPLIANCE.md](docs/COMPLIANCE.md#e-rfc-1006-tcp-profile).
- Limits: `MaxTPDULength` from 128 to 65531, reassembled TSDU 4 MiB by default (`MaxTSDULength`), connect data at most 32 octets. The table with every limit, where it is enforced and the test that pins it is in [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md#resource-limits).
- Memory of a read. `ReadTSDU` keeps the segments received so far in one buffer whose capacity never exceeds `MaxTSDULength`, whatever the size and number of the segments. While that buffer grows, and when the result is handed over, two buffers exist together, so one call holds less than twice `MaxTSDULength`, plus the TPKT packet being read (at most 65535 octets). A partly received TSDU is held until the call returns: until the final segment arrives, the context ends, the stream fails or the connection is closed. A read loop without a deadline therefore keeps it for as long as the peer leaves the TSDU unfinished; multiply by the connections you admit, and lower `MaxTSDULength` where the application protocol allows.
- No handshake timeout of its own. `Connect` and `Accept` wait for the peer for as long as their context allows; pass a context with a deadline ([SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md#resource-limits)).
- A write that times out over TLS. Over a `*tls.Conn` the octets that decide whether a timed-out `WriteTSDU` leaves the connection open are counted above TLS, and crypto/tls fails every write after one that timed out: `WriteTSDU` can return the bare context error and leave the `Conn` open, and the next `WriteTSDU` then fails and aborts it, so treat a write timeout over TLS as the end of the connection.
- The codec's preserving of unknown parameters is for tooling; the service rejects them outside CR.
- Encoders write what they are given. `MarshalBinary` writes CDT as its low 4 bits and TPDU-NR as its low 7 bits without an error for a larger value. `(*DT).MarshalBinary` writes `Parameters` also when `DestinationRef` is nil (X.224 §13.7.1 a) gives the class 0 and 1 format no variable part) and does not refuse a variable part that makes LI odd; `DecodeDT` does not read such TPDUs back as they were built.
- Octets after the header of a DC, ER, AK, EA or RJ, which carry no user data, are ignored by the decoders and not kept.

## Timing

No latency or throughput guarantee. Deadlines come from the caller's context, applied through `net.Conn` deadlines. `make bench` measures locally; no figure is published.

## Not verified

- **Independent COTP peers.** This repository's tests use only this package and test-written raw peers; `testdata/tp0/` fixtures are generated by this package.
- **Indirect use only.** go-s7comm, go-mms and go-iec61850 run their own interop suites through go-cotp against Snap7 and IEC 61850 stacks. These exercise the go-cotp version each of those modules pins in its `go.mod` (v1.3.0 in all three when v1.3.1 was prepared), in the profiles they use, and are not go-cotp evidence for a release.
- **Real devices.** No PLC or IED has been tested from this repository.
- **Platforms.** Tests run on Linux in CI, on amd64 and, nightly, as 32-bit `386` binaries; other platforms are built by `make cross` only.
